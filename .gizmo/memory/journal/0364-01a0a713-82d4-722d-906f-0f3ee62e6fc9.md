## assistant

Tailcat isn't it — but you've almost certainly got the right *shape* of idea, just the wrong name. Here's the distinction:

## Tailcat: not a naming tool

[Tailcat](https://tailscale.com/blog/tailcat) (announced ~2 weeks ago, which is probably why it's on your radar) is "netcat over Tailscale's data plane, without the control plane": no IPs, no accounts, no DNS, no TLS certificates. Both ends run `tailcat` and share an opaque address string out of band to pipe bytes between two machines. There are no hostnames a browser could resolve and no HTTPS certs — it solves ad-hoc machine-to-machine piping, not serving a web app at a nice URL. Dead end for this.

## What you're actually describing: tsnsrv

The trick you want is real, and it's this: a program can join your tailnet as **its own node with its own hostname**, using [tsnet](https://tailscale.com/blog/tsup-tsnet). [tsnsrv](https://github.com/boinkor-net/tsnsrv) (the maintained option — `tsnet-serve` wound itself down in favor of it) is a small reverse proxy that does exactly that:

```
tsnsrv -name gizmo http://127.0.0.1:4173
```

That registers a node called `gizmo` on your tailnet, giving you:

- **`https://gizmo.angler-musical.ts.net`** — a service-named URL, arguably what you wanted all along (not `gizmo.genge`, but `gizmo.<your-tailnet>`)
- Valid Tailscale-issued TLS cert, trusted on every tailnet device, phone included — no CA installs, no DNS-01 challenges, no domain to own
- Tailnet ACLs apply, since it's a first-class node

Costs and caveats, honestly:

- Each service becomes a **node in your admin console** (counts toward device limits depending on plan) — fine for one or two services, noisy if you do it for everything.
- First registration needs a **Tailscale auth key** (env var); tsnsrv then stores a persistent per-service key, so it's a one-time setup, with [OAuth clients](https://tailscale.com/kb/1215/oauth-clients) as the long-lived alternative to expiring keys.
- The same Gizmo-side allowlist change still applies: `https://gizmo.angler-musical.ts.net` must land in Vite preview `allowedHosts` and the agent-server WS origin list.

So the ladder, cheapest first: `tailscale serve` on genge's own name (no new software, machine-scoped URL) → **tsnsrv** (one small binary, service-scoped URL, still zero domain/cert toil) → own-domain-plus-Caddy (full control, but you own DNS and cert renewal). For `https://gizmo.<something>` with the least operational burden, tsnsrv is the sweet spot.

Then let's kill `angler-musical` instead of working around it. You have two ways to never see that name again:

## Option 1: Rename the tailnet (2 minutes, free)

Tailscale lets you change the tailnet name in **Admin console → Settings → General → Tailnet name**. `angler-musical` becomes whatever you actually like — `genge`'s MagicDNS becomes `genge.<your-new-name>.ts.net`, and combined with tsnsrv you'd get `https://gizmo.<your-new-name>.ts.net`.

Caveats, so you're not surprised:
- Old `*.angler-musical.ts.net` names stop resolving — any bookmarks or `tailscale serve` URLs die with it, so you'd re-register serve/tsnsrv after the rename.
- Certs reissue automatically; give DNS a few minutes to settle across devices.
- Every device keeps working, nothing to reinstall — it's just a naming change.

This is by far the least-toil path to pretty URLs, and it fixes the annoyance everywhere, not just for gizmo.

## Option 2: Your own domain (tailnet name never appears, period)

The Caddy recipe from before: public A record `gizmo.<your-domain> → 100.105.88.93`, Caddy with DNS-01 for auto TLS, `reverse_proxy 127.0.0.1:4173` bound to the tailnet IP. Costs a domain (~$10/yr) and one small service on genge, but the tailnet name is nowhere in the picture and certs are publicly trusted.

What I'd skip: running your own private DNS on the tailnet for a custom suffix (split DNS can do it — point a restricted nameserver at a little DNS server on `homelab`, resolve `gizmo.home` or whatever). The names work, but Tailscale won't issue TLS certs for them, so you're back to private-CA toil on every device including your iPhone. Pretty names with broken-HTTPS warnings is worse than an ugly name that just works.

My take: rename the tailnet to something you love, then `tsnsrv -name gizmo` on top of it. Total effort is one settings change plus one tiny binary, and you get `https://gizmo.<name-you-picked>.ts.net` with valid certs on every device.

## assistant

That's exactly the question that decides between the options — and it's where they diverge hard:

## `tailscale serve` on genge: awkward for multiples

One machine, one MagicDNS name. Multiple services means either:

- **Ports**: `https://genge.…:443`, `:8443`, `:8444`… works with zero app changes, but you're back to port juggling — the thing you started out trying to escape. (Plus that stale-registration bug that makes the ports drift upward on restarts.)
- **Paths**: `tailscale serve` can mount backends at subpaths (`/gizmo`, `/photos`). Clean URLs, but SPAs hate living under a subpath — the Vite app would need `base: '/gizmo/'`, the `/agent` proxy mapping becomes `/gizmo/agent`, and the client's socket-URL derivation would need to handle the prefix. Real code changes per service, and every new service repeats them.

## tsnsrv: built for exactly this

Each service joins the tailnet as **its own node**, each on 443, each with its own clean name:

```
tsnsrv -name gizmo  http://127.0.0.1:4173
tsnsrv -name photos http://127.0.0.1:8000
tsnsrv -name files  http://127.0.0.1:9000
```

→ `https://gizmo.<tailnet>.ts.net`, `https://photos.<tailnet>.ts.net`, `https://files.<tailnet>.ts.net`. No ports, no subpaths, no per-app base-path surgery — each app thinks it's served from the root, because it is. This is the setup tsnsrv was written for.

The price is linear: one node per service in your admin console, one tsnsrv process each. For a handful of personal services that's nothing; for fifty it'd be clutter. (Own-domain-plus-Caddy scales the same clean way — `gizmo.example.com`, `photos.example.com` — with one proxy instead of N, at the cost of owning the domain and the cert pipeline.)

So: one service → `tailscale serve` is fine. **More than one → tsnsrv**, no contest. Rename the tailnet to something you don't hate, register each service under its own name, done. Want to start with gizmo as the pilot?

OK. *Now* I get it. You don't want one gizmo — you want the same app on many machines, addressed as `service.machine`: `gizmo.genge`, `gizmo.forge`, `gizmo.outpost`. Service first, machine second.

Here's the thing nobody's said yet: that shape is **backwards from everything Tailscale names natively**. MagicDNS, `tailscale serve`, tsnsrv — they all produce `machine.tailnet` or `service.tailnet`. `service.machine` doesn't exist anywhere in their system, which is why every option so far felt wrong. To get your exact shape, you build one small piece yourself: **split DNS**.

## The recipe for `gizmo.<machine>`

**1. One tiny DNS server on the tailnet.** `homelab` (100.72.105.54, always-on Linux) is the natural host. dnsmasq with literally three lines:

```
address=/gizmo.genge/100.105.88.93
address=/gizmo.forge/100.68.72.45
address=/gizmo.outpost/100.68.130.1
```

**2. Restricted nameservers in Admin console → DNS.** Add `genge`, `forge`, `outpost` as split-DNS domains, all pointing at homelab's tailnet IP. Now *every* tailnet device — your laptop, your iPhone, everything — resolves `gizmo.genge` to genge's 100.x address. No per-device setup, ever. Adding a fourth machine later is one DNS line.

**3. Serve on each machine.** Your `vite preview` already binds all interfaces (`--host`), so it's *already* reachable at `http://100.105.88.93:4173` from the tailnet. Same allowlist change as ever: `gizmo.genge` into Vite `allowedHosts` + the agent-server origin list, per machine.

## The TLS truth you have to pick

A browser opening `https://gizmo.genge` needs a cert *for that name* from something it trusts. Your options are fixed and exhaustive:

- **`http://gizmo.genge`** — zero extra work, and the transport is still WireGuard-encrypted end to end. Downsides: browsers show "Not Secure", and non-`localhost` HTTP isn't a [secure context](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Security/Secure_Contexts) (no `navigator.clipboard`, etc. — worth checking whether the app leans on those).
- **Private CA (mkcert)** — green `https://` everywhere, but you install the CA on every device, and iPhone means the install-profile-then-enable-full-trust dance. One-time per device, annoying exactly once.
- There is no third option. Tailscale only issues certs for `*.ts.net` names, and no public CA will issue for `genge`.

## Quick win for today

Before any of that: a hosts-file line on your own laptop (`100.105.88.93 gizmo.genge`) gives you `http://gizmo.genge` in 30 seconds to *feel* the shape. Phones won't see it, but you'll know whether the dream is worth the infra.
